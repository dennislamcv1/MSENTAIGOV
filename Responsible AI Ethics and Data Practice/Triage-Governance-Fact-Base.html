Designated most critical finding — BF-01

A false negative in triage is an under-triage: an acutely ill patient assigned a lower acuity than their condition warrants. That is the direct pathway to the delayed-care and mortality harm named in the impact assessment. Patients 70+ are the department's highest-acuity cohort, so an identical disparity carries greater absolute harm.

BF-02 and BF-03 describe unequal prioritisation rates. BF-01 describes missed critical illness. Sections 1, 2 and 5 must all escalate on BF-01, and BF-01 must carry an acceptance criterion — at present the escalated finding has none while the unescalated one does.

Why BF-05 is P2

It breaches the 0.05 threshold and so requires documented remediation, but does not exceed the 0.10 P1 ceiling. It is the package's only P2, and it exists so learners must actually apply the two-tier rule instead of marking everything P1.

Retired metrics and why: Equalized Odds 0.27 — FNR disparity is its clinically meaningful half; carrying both double-counts one finding. False Positive Rate Disparity 0.18 — describes over-triage, which contradicts the delayed-care harm narrative running through every other document.

Aggregation rule — the missing sentence

Any Critical Flag or any Fail sets the minimum decision at Mitigate. Reject is reserved for violations with no viable remediation path. A Critical Flag or Fail halts execution pending remediation; it does not by itself force Reject.

This requires one edit to the scoring key. Critical Flag becomes “significant risk requiring execution halt pending remediation”, replacing “may not be addressable through standard mitigation” — the wording that currently contradicts the Mitigate decision it sits beside.

Monitoring statement — merged spec. Every monitoring statement in the package states five things: what is tracked, at what cadence, by whom, the trigger threshold that constitutes a breach, and the escalation path when it breaches. The Section 2 brief currently asks for the first three; the compliance checklist asks for a different three. This is the union.
