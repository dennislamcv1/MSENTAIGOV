PROMPT 2 — INTEGRATION RATIONALE
AI Risk & Security Strategy Report — Contoso Financial Service GenAI Knowledge Assistant


The most important consistency decision was to model the FAIR scenario as a chained
loss path rather than a single risk.

The supplied calculator quantified prompt injection (R-05) with a secondary loss band
reaching $11,000,000 in regulatory penalty. That figure could not survive the scope
boundary set in Section 2, which excludes customer personal data from the indexed
corpus. Against a correctly bounded corpus, a successful injection extracts internal
policy content — sensitive, but not a notifiable breach at anything near that scale.
Two sections were quietly contradicting each other.

I resolved it by making R-01, ingestion boundary failure, the enabling precondition of
the quantified scenario: the loss magnitude is credible only where excluded content is
already in the index, and injection is the mechanism that extracts it.

That decision then propagated. Sensitivity analysis showed ingestion controls cutting
modelled maximum exposure by roughly 73 per cent against 60 per cent for endpoint
controls. This is why Section 5 flags the complete absence of DLP capability as the
highest-leverage gap, why R-01 outranks prompt injection in the prioritization, and why
Section 7 names ingestion boundary enforcement as one of only two conditions whose
failure converts conditional approval into deferral.

It strengthened defensibility because the number now carries a stated mechanism. A
committee member asking why a fine-scale figure attaches to an internal-only system
gets an answer, rather than exposing an inherited assumption nobody had tested.
